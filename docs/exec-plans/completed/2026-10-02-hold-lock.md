# v1.358: the critter flake, then drag down while holding to lock the 2x

Drafted 2026-10-02 by the Sonnet builder-session (v1.357) at main e6840db5. Refined 2026-10-03 by the Architect (Opus): Wave Z
added, every seam re-read at e6840db5, five draft defects fixed (section 9 lists them), Dean's rulings folded in. ROADMAP Planned
"Idea: lock the hold-to-speed-up by dragging down" is the WHAT; this plan is the HOW. One gesture in `public/js/player.js`, one
pill state, one icon, no server, no storage, no new dependency.

## 1. The outcome (what Dean will do on device)

1. Phone, Settings > Mobile player > "Use custom player controls on touch devices" ON (the hold gesture does not run under the
   native video controls, see section 4), a playing video in full screen: press and hold the picture, 2x starts (today). Without
   lifting, drag the finger DOWN a short way: the 2x pill gains a lock mark and the finger can lift: playback STAYS at 2x.
2. While locked, taps on the picture behave as always (tap to pause or show the bar, double-tap to skip 15 s) and the lock stays.
   One tap on the pill unlocks it and playback returns to the speed it had before the hold.
3. A plain hold (no drag down) is unchanged: 2x while held, normal speed on lift. A hold that drags sideways or up behaves as today.
   Swipe-back and the double-tap skip are not disturbed. The lock ends on the next item, on dock or close, on entering native
   fullscreen, on an explicit speed pick, and when the app goes to the background (Dean 2026-10-03).

## 3. Research to do first (W0 measures; nothing here is assumed)

1. **Does a `touchmove` keep arriving at `#media-player` / `#audio-bg-art` after the 500 ms hold latches**, in headless Chromium
   with touch emulation (CDP `Input.dispatchTouchEvent`), and with `e.cancelable` true or false? Today the listener is
   `passive: true` and returns early on `holdActive`, so nothing reads the drag.
2. **Scroll and refresh during a hold-drag, inline and in faux fullscreen.** Record `scrollY` before and after a 600 ms hold plus
   an 80 px downward drag, at `scrollY` 0 and at `scrollY` > 0. Record the computed `touch-action` of both surfaces and their
   ancestors (`body` is `manipulation` from ui.css ~59; nothing on the surfaces today: `grep -n "touch-action" public/css/*.css`).
   Then measure the three candidate mechanisms, cheapest first, and keep only one that MEASURES effective (an unmeasured
   mechanism is an inert feature, LESSONS 2):
   - (a) a `hold-active` class that sets `touch-action: none` on the surface at engage. Expected INERT: engines resolve
     touch-action at touchstart, so a mid-gesture change likely does nothing. Measure it, record it, drop it if inert.
   - (b) a non-passive `touchmove` listener on the surface, ADDED at touchstart and removed by the gesture's own end
     (touchend/touchcancel/releaseHold: the state-nuller is the sole remover, LESSONS 4 v1.160.1), that calls `preventDefault()`
     only while `holdActive`. Measure whether Chromium delivers the post-latch touchmoves as cancelable when the listener was
     added after a passive touchstart.
   - (c) the surface's existing `touchmove` registered `passive: false` permanently, early-returning unless `holdActive`.
     Element-scoped, never global (LESSONS 4: a global non-passive touch listener is a scroll-perf regression). Measure the
     scroll-start latency cost on the surface if you choose it, and state the trade in section 6.
   Headless Chromium has no pull-to-refresh and is not WebKit (LESSONS 8): an effective mechanism in Chromium proves only
   Chromium. iOS Safari (tab and home-screen app) is Dean's device check (section 7). Per R6, if no candidate stops the inline
   scroll in Chromium, inline is out and the lock ships full screen only.
3. **Swipe-back and the app's own pull-to-refresh.** Swipe-back (common.js ~10399-10530) claims only a clearly horizontal drag
   (`swipeBackShouldClaim`: dx > 16 and |dx| > 1.5 |dy|), and it stays live in the expanded audio view (v1.311.3). Bind R1's lock
   region against it as pure-by-pure: for every input `holdDragDecision` calls `'lock'`, `swipeBackShouldClaim` is false. The
   app's pull-to-refresh (main.js ~2919, home view, window listener gated on `ptrIndicator.isConnected`, threshold 70 px) should be
   inert on a page that hosts the full player: measure it (home cached behind the watch page), do not assume it.
4. **The background-audio handoff order (found at refinement, read at e6840db5).** `handleBackgroundLifecycle` registers its
   pagehide / freeze / visibility-hidden listeners at ~4622-4630, BEFORE `resetGestureLatchesOnBackground` (~4688-4691). On
   hidden, the handoff copies `mediaPlayer.playbackRate` onto the sidecar (`carriedRate`, ~3513) FIRST, then the safety net
   restores only the video element. So a hold that is live at backgrounding plays the background audio at 2x today (a latent
   bug for a plain hold, the COMMON case for a lock), against Dean's R8 ruling. Measure it in jsdom with the real listeners
   (experimental background audio eligible, a held or locked 2x, dispatch visibility hidden, read the sidecar's rate), then fix
   in W1.6. `handlePossibleIOSPrePauseHandoff` (~3627) is the second handoff trigger: cover it too.
5. **The touchend and touchcancel paths** (the draft had this wrong, section 9 D2). Today `touchend` with `holdActive` does
   `preventDefault()` + `releaseHold()` + `lastTapTime = 0` + return, and `touchcancel` and a multi-finger `touchstart` call
   `releaseHold()`. With a lock, `holdActive` stays true across LATER gestures, so every one of these would either release the
   lock or swallow the tap. The hold branches must key on the gesture that ENGAGED the hold (`holdGestureLive`), not on
   `holdActive`.
6. **Which audio surfaces reach the gesture on the phone.** `#audio-bg-art` gets `wireSkipHoldGestures`; the full-screen music
   skins (`body.mms-on`, `.mms-full` with `touch-action: none`, style.css ~8243) cover the viewport. List which phone audio paths
   (Music with and without a skin, Podcasts, an audio file on the watch page) actually let a hold reach `#audio-bg-art`, and word
   device check 2 for one that does.
